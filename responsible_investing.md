# Responsible investing: The ESG-efficient frontier

**Five-fold conclusion:**
1. The investor optimally chooses a porfolio from the ESG-efficient frontier
2. Four-fund separation: The portfolios that span the frontier are a combination of the risk free asset, the tangency portfolio, the minimum-variance portfolio and the ESG-tangency portfolio
3. Equilibrium asset returns satisfy an ESG-adjusted CAPM, showing whenESG assets have lower or higher equilibrium returns
4. We estimate the costs and benefits of responsible investing via the empirical ESG-efficient frontier based on E, S and G measuresand show how ESG screens can have surprising effects
5. We test the theory's equilibrium predictions using four ESG proxies

We consider 3 types of investors:
1. **Type-U (ESG-unaware)**: investors are unavare of ESG scores and simply seek to maximize their unconventional mean-variance utility
2. **Type-A (ESG-aware)**: investors also have mean-variance utility, but they use asset's ESG scores to update their views of risk and expected return
3. **Type-M (ESG-motivated)**: investors use ESG scores and also have preferences for high ESG scores

This can seem challenging, but the investor's problem can be simplified to a trade-off between ESG and Sharpe ratio  
Specifically, for each level of ESG, we compute the highest attainable SR. We denote this connection between ESG score and SR as the ESG-SR frontier  
**This depends only on security characteristics; thus it is independent from investor preferences!**
